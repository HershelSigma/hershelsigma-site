GoHighLevel dialer: what is built in, and when to add a predictive dialer

By Nathan Yonash, Founder, Hershel Sigma AI · Updated September 29, 2026

GoHighLevel's built-in dialer is a browser softphone plus a Power Dialer that lets a rep work through a workflow-built queue of call tasks, typically over LC Phone, HighLevel's native phone system. It suits small teams and one-to-one calling. Floors that need predictive, multi-line dialing with managed pacing and caller ID rotation usually pair GoHighLevel with a dedicated dialer.

Plenty of appointment-setting agencies run their whole business inside GoHighLevel: funnels, pipelines, calendars and follow-up. When the floor grows, the question is whether HighLevel's own calling can carry it. This guide covers what the built-in tools do today, where they fit, and how a dedicated dialer works beside GoHighLevel without replacing it.

What calling does GoHighLevel have built in?

GoHighLevel has two calling tools, both documented in HighLevel's help center (checked September 29, 2026). Calls typically run over LC Phone, which HighLevel describes as its proprietary telephony service provider.

  • Web Dialer. A browser softphone. The rep picks a contact or types a number, chooses an eligible caller ID, and can use call scripts, notes and tags during or after the call (HighLevel help: outbound calls with the web dialer, updated September 4, 2026).
  • Power Dialer. A workflow's Manual Call action creates call tasks under Conversations, Manual Actions. The rep clicks "Let's Start" and works through that queue task by task (Manual Actions). HighLevel's help center is explicit that the Web Dialer and the Power Dialer are not the same thing.
  • Call records. Calls can be recorded, and LC Phone calls can produce a transcript and summary once transcription is enabled in phone settings (Transcript Generated trigger, updated February 19, 2026).
  • Number reputation. Voice Integrity submits eligible U.S. numbers to First Orion, Hiya and TNS for reputation review (Voice Integrity, updated September 11, 2026).

For a small team calling its own funnel leads, that is a solid setup: calls happen inside the same CRM that holds the lead, the pipeline and the calendar.

Does GoHighLevel have a predictive dialer?

Not according to HighLevel's own documentation as of September 29, 2026. The Power Dialer is built for a rep working a queue of call tasks, rather than for a system that dials several numbers per rep and hands the rep whoever answers. None of the help articles above describe multi-line dialing, and a request for a parallel dialer is still marked open on HighLevel's public ideas board.

That is fine for warm, inbound leads. The limits appear on high-volume outbound floors, in three places:

  • Talk time. On cold or aged lists, most dials ring out or reach voicemail. With one line per rep, the rep waits through every one of them. A predictive dialer dials ahead of the reps so the next free one gets a person who picked up.
  • Pacing. Dialing several lines per rep needs a governor. Too slow and reps sit idle; too fast and answered calls find no free rep and drop. Someone, or something, has to watch drop rate and agent wait time all shift.
  • Caller ID wear. Heavy outbound volume wears numbers down. HighLevel's own spam guidance says registration does not guarantee a number stays free of spam labels, and it treats replacing a number as a case-by-case decision (HighLevel help: calls marked as spam, updated September 8, 2026).

Floors at that stage usually look for a GoHighLevel power dialer alternative: a predictive or AI dialer that does the dialing while GoHighLevel keeps doing what it does well.

How Hershel works with GoHighLevel

Hershel runs the outbound dialing and GoHighLevel stays the CRM and calendar. Reps dial from Hershel's own web rep console, managers watch the floor, campaigns and lists in the Hershel Sigma dashboard, and appointments still live on your GoHighLevel calendars. The connection between the two covers three things:

  1. A closer brief on every booked contact. Every appointment-set call is transcribed and turned into a closer brief, posted as a note on the contact in GoHighLevel before the appointment.
  2. A calendar sweep. Hershel sweeps the GoHighLevel booking calendars for any booking without a brief and briefs it from the booked call, so a booking that slipped past the first pass still gets one.
  3. No double-dialing a booked lead. Once a booking is briefed, that lead is taken out of dialing, so a second setter never calls a homeowner who already booked.

The brief only includes what the prospect said, not what the setter pitched, and it is built to be read in about 20 seconds before the call. Here is what one looks like on a contact.

Example closer brief (fictional homeowner)

ONE-LINER: Linda H., homeowner in Tampa, FL. Shared decision maker: her husband Dave will be on the appointment. Close probability 7/10: both are frustrated with rising bills and asked real questions about batteries.

PAIN POINTS:

  • Summer electric bills around $380 a month and climbing
  • Lost power for two days after the last storm

UVPs MENTIONED:

  • Perked up at a fixed monthly payment instead of rising rates
  • Asked twice about battery backup during outages

CONCERNS & OBJECTIONS:

  • Roof is about 12 years old; worried about leaks
  • A neighbor had a bad experience with another installer

SCHEDULING NOTES:

  • Thursday at 6:00 pm, both home
  • Dave gets off at 5:30; confirm by text with Linda

How Hershel runs the dialing side

Hershel is an AI dialer built on a proven open-source dialing engine, then rebuilt around AI: its own rep console, its own manager screens, and AI that runs dial pace, caller IDs and call notes.

  • Dialler Manager reads live campaign stats about every 10 seconds and adjusts the dial level so reps talk to as many people as possible while the drop rate stays under the ceiling the floor sets.
  • The Shield watches every outbound caller ID for spam flags and carrier refusals, including channel-limit and calls-per-second refusals that ordinary call records hide, and pages a manager only when a human has to act.
  • Closable writes the closer briefs that land in GoHighLevel.

Each client gets its own dedicated dialer server, and Hershel runs as a fully managed service, 24/7.

When GoHighLevel's built-in dialer is enough

The built-in tools are enough when calling is a follow-up step, not the core of the operation. That usually looks like:

  • A few reps calling leads that came in from forms, ads or funnels.
  • Short, warm lists where most people expect the call.
  • Calls that are one step inside a longer GoHighLevel workflow of texts and emails.

A dedicated dialer earns its place when these sound familiar instead:

  • A floor of setters dialing large cold or aged lists for appointments every day.
  • Reps spending more of the hour waiting on rings than talking.
  • Numbers getting flagged faster than anyone has time to swap them.
  • Closers walking into appointments without knowing what the setter heard.

Hershel was built and battle-tested on solar appointment-setting floors in Texas and Florida, where all four are everyday problems.
