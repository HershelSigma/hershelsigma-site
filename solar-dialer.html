AI dialer for solar appointment setting

By Nathan Yonash, Founder, Hershel Sigma AI · Updated September 29, 2026

An AI dialer for solar is a predictive dialer whose pace, caller IDs and call notes are run by AI, built for homeowner lists with low answer rates and long qualifying calls. It keeps setters talking, rotates out flagged numbers, briefs the closer on every booking and keeps booked homeowners out of the dial pool.

Hershel was built and battle-tested on outbound solar appointment-setting floors in Texas and Florida run by its founder, Nathan Yonash, and this guide is written from that floor. It is about AI that runs the dialer around human setters, not bots that talk to homeowners. For the general definition, see what an AI dialer is.

Why solar appointment setting is hard on a dialer

Solar setting mixes cold homeowner lists with long qualifying conversations, and that mix breaks dialer settings built for short calls.

  • Low answer rates. Homeowners on a purchased or aged list did not ask for the call, so the dialer has to place a lot of calls to keep each setter busy.
  • Real conversations. The setter asks about the utility bill, the roof, shading and who else is on the title. Talk times run long and swing widely.
  • A handoff to someone who was not there. The closer never heard the set call, so anything not written down is lost.
  • Numbers that burn. Hard dialing puts heavy volume on every caller ID. A flagged number stops getting answered, and that looks a lot like a bad list.

What a solar floor actually needs from a dialer

A solar floor needs four things from its dialer, and all four have to hold up at full volume:

  1. Pace that keeps setters talking, without long waits or a pile of dropped calls.
  2. Caller IDs that keep getting answered, with flagged numbers pulled fast.
  3. A clean handoff to the closer, carrying what the homeowner said.
  4. No double-booking the same homeowner once they are on the calendar.

How an AI dialer handles each one

An AI dialer handles these by watching the floor continuously and acting on what it sees, instead of waiting for a manager to notice.

Pace that keeps setters talking

Pace on a solar floor is a moving target. Long bill-and-roof calls tie setters up unpredictably, then several free up at once. A pace set at the start of the shift ends up too slow when answers are thin and too fast when every setter is mid-call, which drops live homeowners.

An AI dialer reads drop rate, agent wait time, pause time and leads ready to dial, and moves the dial level as they change. Hershel's Dialler Manager does this about every 10 seconds, pushing for as many conversations as possible while it keeps the drop rate (drops divided by answered calls) under the ceiling the floor sets.

Caller IDs that keep getting answered

Answer rate lives or dies on the caller ID. Hiya's 2026 State of the Call report found that 86% of calls from unknown numbers go unanswered, and a number with a spam label does worse. One burned number can quietly drag down a campaign while everyone blames the list.

An AI dialer watches each number on its own, not just the campaign average. Hershel's Shield checks every caller ID for spam flags and carrier refusals, pulls and replaces a flagged number, and pages a manager only when a human has to act. It works with Twilio and Telnyx, and it catches channel-limit and calls-per-second refusals that ordinary call records hide.

A clean handoff to the closer

In a busy shift, setters skip notes or type "interested, call Tues", and the closer walks in cold. An AI dialer closes that gap by transcribing the appointment-set call and turning it into a brief. Hershel's Closable posts that brief as a note on the homeowner's contact in GoHighLevel before the appointment. It includes only what the homeowner said, not what the setter pitched, and is built to be read in about 20 seconds.

No double-booking the same homeowner

Solar leads get dialed more than once: callbacks, recycled lists, the same household on two lists. A booked homeowner left in the dial pool can get a second call, or a second appointment the closer never hears about. Hershel sweeps the booking calendars for any appointment without a brief and briefs it from the booked call. Once a booking is briefed, the lead comes out of dialing, so a second setter never calls a booked homeowner.

What to look for when choosing a solar dialer

Judge a solar dialer by how it behaves at full volume on a cold homeowner list. Bring these questions to every demo:

  1. Who sets the pace mid-shift? It should adjust itself from live drop rate and agent wait, inside a ceiling you choose. Ask how drop rate is calculated.
  2. Does it watch each caller ID? Campaign averages hide a single burned number. Look for per-number monitoring and automatic replacement.
  3. Can it see carrier refusals? Channel-limit and calls-per-second refusals often pass as normal failed calls.
  4. How do notes reach the closer? The strongest answer is a brief built from the recording, in the CRM your closers already use, before the appointment.
  5. What stops a booked homeowner from being called again? Booking and dialing should be linked, not left to setters.
  6. Who runs it? Ask if the server is dedicated or shared, and who watches it around the clock.
  7. How does pricing scale? Per-seat pricing grows with every setter. A flat rate tied to floor size is easier to plan around.
  8. When does it alert a human? Only when someone has to act. Noisy alerts get muted.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best dialer for solar appointment setting?

The best dialer for solar appointment setting keeps setters talking on low-answer homeowner lists, keeps caller IDs from burning, gets every booking to the closer with notes, and never redials a booked homeowner. That means predictive dialing with AI running pace, numbers and call notes, which Hershel was built on solar floors to do.

Should a solar floor use a predictive or a power dialer?

A power dialer is fine for a few setters on warm leads. Once several setters work cold homeowner lists, predictive dialing usually wins because it dials ahead of free setters instead of waiting for them. Predictive pace needs constant tuning, which is the job an AI dialer takes over.

How do solar floors stop their numbers getting flagged as spam?

No floor can rule out flags completely, but it can limit the damage: watch every outbound caller ID on its own, pull a number as soon as it picks up a spam label or carrier refusals, and replace it. Hershel's Shield does this automatically and alerts a manager only when a person needs to act.
