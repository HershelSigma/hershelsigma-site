What is a managed dialer?

By Nathan Yonash, Founder, Hershel Sigma AI · Updated September 29, 2026

A managed dialer is an outbound dialer that the vendor runs for you. The provider hosts the server, connects the carriers, manages caller IDs, tunes the dial pace, watches number health and handles updates and monitoring. Your floor brings the agents and the leads, and the vendor keeps the dialer calling.

Buying a dialer is the easy part. Running one well is a daily job: keeping the server healthy, keeping carriers connected, retiring flagged caller IDs and adjusting the dial pace as answer rates move. A managed dialer service hands that job to the vendor. The label gets used loosely, so this guide pins it down, compares the options and lists what to ask a provider.

What makes a dialer "managed"

A dialer is managed when the vendor does the operating work, not just the hosting. The dialing underneath can be the same predictive or power dialing any floor uses. What changes is who is responsible when something drifts. You will also see it called outsourced dialer management or a dialer management service, and a fully managed predictive dialer is simply a predictive dialer with that operating work included.

The operating work usually covers five areas:

  • Server: hosting, capacity, backups and updates that do not break a campaign mid-shift.
  • Carriers: the phone trunks that carry the calls, and the calls those trunks refuse.
  • Caller IDs: buying numbers, rotating them and retiring the ones that get flagged as spam.
  • Dial pace: how many lines to dial per agent as the list, the hour and the headcount change.
  • Monitoring: watching all of the above whenever the floor is open, and fixing problems before agents feel them.

Hosted is not the same as managed: a hosted dialer lives on the vendor's servers, but your team still runs it. When AI handles the pace and number work inside a managed service, the result is a managed AI dialer. The guide to what an AI dialer is explains that control loop.

Managed dialer vs self-run and seat-based dialers

Outbound floors run their dialing one of three ways, and the real difference is who does the daily operating work and how you pay for it.

Each option fits a different floor. A self-run dialer gives you the most control and the smallest software bill, and suits teams with an in-house admin who knows the dialer and the carriers. The cost is time: every flagged number and slow morning lands on that person. A seat-based cloud dialer suits small teams that want to start fast and budget per head, though settings and numbers stay your job and the bill grows with every seat. A managed AI dialer suits floors where talk time is the product: when idle agents and burned numbers cost money every hour, paying a provider to run the dialer can cost less than running it yourself.

What a managed dialer should include

A complete managed dialer covers the whole operating job, and you can hold any provider against this checklist:

  • A dedicated server. Your dialer should run on a server set aside for your floor, so another client's call volume cannot slow your campaigns.
  • 24/7 monitoring. The dialer, carriers and numbers should be watched around the clock, not only when you open a ticket.
  • Pace tuning. The dial level should change through the shift based on drop rate and agent wait time, and hold the drop rate under the ceiling the floor sets. Drop rate here means drops divided by answered calls.
  • Caller ID rotation. Numbers that pick up spam flags or get refused should come out of rotation and be replaced before answer rates sag.
  • Carrier monitoring. Carriers refuse calls at their own channel and calls-per-second limits, and those refusals can look like ordinary failed calls in standard reports. The provider should catch them.
  • Call notes to your CRM. Booked appointments should reach your CRM with notes on what the prospect said, so closers walk in prepared and setters keep dialing.
  • Alerts only when a human must act. A text for every small change trains managers to ignore alerts. Routine fixes should happen quietly.
  • Clear pricing. You should know the setup fee, the monthly rate and what would change it before you buy.

Questions to ask a managed dialer provider

Good questions reveal who actually does the work after go-live:

  1. Is my dialer server dedicated to my floor, or shared with other clients? On shared capacity, someone else's busy morning can become yours.
  2. Who changes the dial pace during the day, and what limit does it respect? Ask how they calculate drop rate. Drops divided by answered calls is a stricter measure than drops divided by all dials.
  3. What happens when a caller ID gets flagged? Find out who notices, how quickly the number is replaced and who buys the replacement.
  4. How do you catch carrier refusals that do not show up in call records? A provider that only reads dialer reports can miss a carrier quietly throwing calls away.
  5. What will my managers be alerted about, and what do you handle without asking? You want few alerts, each one tied to a decision.
  6. Do I keep control of my campaigns, lists and data? The floor should decide what gets dialed and when, and be able to export its lists.
  7. What is in the setup fee, what is in the monthly rate, and what changes the rate? Floor size and call volume are fair drivers. Surprise add-ons are not.

Frequently asked questions

What is a managed dialer?

A managed dialer is an outbound dialer that the vendor operates for you. The provider hosts the server, connects the carriers, manages caller IDs, tunes the dial pace and monitors the system, so your team supplies the agents, the leads and the campaign decisions.

What is the difference between a managed dialer and a hosted dialer?

A hosted dialer only moves the software onto the vendor's servers. Your team still sets the pace, buys and rotates numbers and troubleshoots when calls stop connecting. A managed dialer includes that operating work: the vendor runs the dialer day to day, not just the server it lives on.

Is a managed dialer more expensive than seat-based software?

It depends on floor size and what your team's time is worth. Seat-based software charges per agent per month and leaves the tuning and number work with you. A managed dialer usually charges a setup fee plus a flat monthly rate, so compare the full cost, including manager hours spent running the dialer and talk time lost when it runs badly.

Do I keep control of my campaigns and lists?

You should. The floor decides which lists to dial, the campaign hours, the scripts and the drop-rate ceiling, and the provider runs the dialer inside those limits. With Hershel, managers see and manage the floor, campaigns and lists in the Hershel Sigma dashboard.

What does the floor still do with a managed dialer?

The floor brings the agents and the leads, sets the goals and limits, and does the selling. Reps log in and take calls. Managers coach, choose which lists and campaigns run, and act on the few alerts that need a human decision.

How fast can a managed dialer go live?

It depends mostly on how ready the floor is. The provider builds the server and handles carriers, numbers and dialer setup, so the floor mainly needs its lead lists, reps, campaign rules and CRM access ready. Ask for a go-live plan during the demo.
